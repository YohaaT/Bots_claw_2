Video: Ce que j'aurais voulu savoir avant de me lancer dans le trading
Source: user-provided transcript

Key lessons extracted
- Trading is a métier, not a game.
- Psychology matters, but only if biases are identified concretely and reviewed trade by trade.
- Review trades the next day, not hot/immediately after.
- Do not use naked signals; context matters.
- Do not wait systematically for confirmation/pullback; often the move leaves without you.
- Never average down to avoid validating a loss.
- Do not move stop loss farther to avoid pain.
- Limit consecutive losses and stop trading for the session/day when needed.
- Use minimal leverage, especially as beginner.
- Money management does not create edge; it protects capital.
- Avoid indicator overload; simple, assimilated methods are preferred.
- One strategy does not fit all market states.
- Need different handling for:
  - trend start,
  - trend continuation,
  - impulse,
  - trend exhaustion/exaggeration,
  - opening session / event-driven contexts.
- Exits are neglected and must be studied explicitly.
- Relative strength is highlighted as a major practical edge.

Configurations / practical implications for our research
1. Architecture preference confirmed:
   signal + context + exit + risk management
2. Strong recommendation to prioritize relative strength as top-level directional/context filter.
3. Strong recommendation against over-combining indicators.
4. Market-regime adaptation is required; methods must be regime-aware.
5. Session risk rule candidate:
   - stop after 2 losing trades in half-day or 3 in full day (example from speaker, not universal rule)
6. Prefer a small number of well-understood modules over large indicator stacks.

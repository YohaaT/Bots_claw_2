Core directives extracted
- Treat trading as a métier/profession, not as a hobby.
- A strategy is not enough; the operator must understand rules, safety, protocols, and market context.
- Do not expect to become competent quickly; no instant mastery.
- Every method must be used only in its favorable environment.
- Avoid averaging down; a loss remains a loss even if later compensated elsewhere.
- Do not move stop-loss away to avoid pain.
- Money management protects capital; it does not create profits by itself.
- Optimizing only stop/target geometry is not enough; filters/context are needed.
- Exits should not rely on hope; trailing/exit logic must be explicit and understood.
- Market understanding should be built from flows and structure, not slogans like 'more buyers than sellers'.
- Support/resistance, acceleration, action-reaction, and range structure should be interpreted through participant imbalance.
- Fewer well-understood indicators are preferable to overloaded screens.
- Each market state may require a different method.
- Preserve continuity of practice; long interruptions degrade execution skill.
- Never adopt an indicator or technique in real trading without verifying/testing it first.

Learning sequence / bot training sequence implied
1. Learn market structure interpretation.
2. Learn to recognize entry structures.
3. Learn to recognize exit structures.
4. Understand the indicators used and how they are built.
5. Demo / paper training.
6. Real trading with very small size.
7. Continuous review of losing trades.
8. Gradual size increase only after stability.
9. Permanent search for better context filters and execution discipline.

Actionable directives for future bot
- Bot should prefer context-aware trades over naked signals.
- Bot should tag market regime before evaluating an entry.
- Bot should record and analyze losing trades more deeply than winning trades.
- Bot should avoid signal overload and prefer simple modular combinations.
- Bot should support separate modules for:
  - structure/context,
  - entry,
  - exit,
  - session/volatility filters,
  - risk sizing.
- Bot should treat relative strength and structural context as high-priority filters.
- Bot should not infer that a profitable backtest from one regime generalizes to all regimes.
- Bot should track consecutive losses and allow session/day stop rules.

Research consequences
- Prioritize research on signal + filter combinations, not raw entries only.
- Evaluate robustness across different market regimes.
- Explicitly test whether exits/stops are doing the real heavy lifting.
- Use modular backtests that separate:
  entry edge,
  context filter edge,
  exit edge,
  risk sizing effects.
